# How skin is bound to fat, and through fat to deeper layers

Desk research, 2026-11-24. Nothing was downloaded or installed. *unverified* means one of three things: I could not read the primary source, the claim came from a secondary snippet, or it is my own reading.

**Answer for the handoff (§6b / table row "fat ↔ skin"):** the "skin slides only a few mm" assumption is **half right**.
- The skin moves together with its superficial fat and the superficial fascia, within about 1 mm (Wilke & Tenberg 2020).
- Relative to **bone**, thigh skin moves 7–14 mm RMS while walking and peaks at 23–72 mm (Cereatti et al. 2017).
- So the motion is real and large, but it happens deeper: the deep fat slides over the deep fascia, muscle slides under the fascia, and the skin stretches. The septa explain the tight coupling of skin to superficial fascia. They do not explain the movement of skin relative to bone.

## 1. Anatomy

### 1.1 The layer stack

In living people, 3T MRI shows the same stack in every body region: **skin → superficial adipose tissue (SAT) → stratum membranosum / superficial fascia (SM) → deep adipose tissue (DAT) → deep fascia → muscle**. Two networks of fibrous septa cross the fat:
- **Retinacula cutis superficialis (RCS)**, from the dermis to the SM, roughly **perpendicular** to the skin.
- **Retinacula cutis profundus (RCP)**, from the SM to the deep fascia, more **oblique** and "parallel supports".

The fat forms lobules in this honeycomb. Skin ligaments are local densifications where the RCS and RCP line up and run straight from the deep plane to the dermis. (Herlin et al. 2015, 6 volunteers, whole-body 3T MRI plus segmentation. DOI [10.1007/s00276-014-1409-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00276-014-1409-0))

Other measurements from the same paper:
- Skin thickness: 3 mm on the thorax, 5 mm on the anterior abdomen, 7 mm on the lumbar abdomen.
- Thighs and arms have 1–2 SM layers. Forearms and legs have one.

The dermis–hypodermis junction itself is described as a continuation of dermal collagen into the septa. The septa are said to be compositionally comparable to dermal collagen. That comes from a secondary review ([ResearchSquare rs-8467471](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-8467471/latest.pdf), preprint), so it is *unverified*.

### 1.3 Tight vs loose tethering

- **Gluteal fold, medial part:** a "fascial condensation zone". This is an "extremely dense and tough" retinaculum cutis **anchored to the ischium** and inserted radially into the dermis. It fades laterally into a fat-dominant two-layer superficial fascial system, and the fold disappears into a smooth buttock–thigh curve. (Si et al. 2024, PRS 154:241e, 20 fresh female buttocks dissected, DOI [10.1097/PRS.0000000000010723](https://doi.org/10.1097/PRS.0000000000010723))
- **Ischio-cutaneous ligament:** on the medial side of the gluteal fold, a strong band runs from the ischial tuberosity into the gluteus maximus and the dermis. Found in 37 formalin-fixed cadavers plus 1 soft-fixed cadaver ([Kurume repository PDF](https://kurume.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2000172/files/37104B3059.pdf); Clin Anat 2023 per snippet, *unverified*).
- **Infragluteal fold:** has a fibrous component with bony origins. MRI and cadaver measurements of those origins agree (Stivala, di Summa et al. 2021; abstract only, no numbers, *unverified*).
- **Zones of adherence** (Lockwood 1991: gluteal crease, lateral gluteal depression, distal lateral thigh, medial knee, etc.): widely cited, but I did not read the paper. *unverified*.
- **Loose areas:** where there is a thick sub-SM fat layer and RCP are sparse, for example the lateral thigh and the lateral buttock (Herlin 2015). In flap surgery, raising the flap below the SM leaves the skin "anchored tightly" and avoids "gliding". This implies the gliding plane sits **below** the SM (Herlin 2015, Discussion).
- **Knee crease:** no study of septa or ligaments specific to the popliteal or anterior knee crease was found. This is a gap.

What the table shows:
- STA mixes several things: skin sliding, skin stretch, muscle contraction and wobbling mass (Cereatti 2017).
- In the Wilke data, the superficial fascia hardly moves relative to the skin probe while the muscle moves 5.7 mm. My reading (*unverified*): skin, SAT and SM form one unit, and the slip happens at the deep fascia and muscle.
- Thigh STA is largest proximally and near the knee (Stacey 2005; Akbarshahi 2010).

### 2.3 Dimples, folds and creases

- **Gluteal fold:** a fold forms where the RC is dense and bone-anchored (medial). Where the superficial fascial system is fat-dominant (lateral), you get a smooth curve instead (Si 2024). The ischio-cutaneous band pins the dermis to the ischium.
- **Cellulite-type dimpling:** fat bulges between perpendicular septa (honeycomb lobules, Herlin 2015). I found no quantitative dimple-depth data, so this is a gap.
- **Knee:** knee skin has "lines of non-extension" during squats: lines along which the skin keeps a constant length (motion-capture strain field, Wessendorf & Newman 2012, IEEE TBME 59:3432; strain values not read, *unverified*).

## 6. Recommended skin-binding model for the engine

1. **Treat skin + SAT + SM as one bound sheet.**
   - The skin membrane is embedded in the outer fat tets: barycentric, zero slip.
   - The SM is a thin stiff shell, tied to the SAT.
   - Basis: superficial-fascia slip of 0.72 ± 0.81 mm (Wilke 2020); RCS run perpendicular (Herlin 2015).
2. **Put the sliding plane at the deep fat / deep fascia.**
   - Contact between the DAT and the fascia lata with low friction.
   - The RCP become **tension-only tethers** from SM vertices to deep-fascia anchor points.
   - Tether rest length = local DAT thickness (geometry from the warped atlas). Density per region follows Li & Ahn (thigh > calf > arm) and Herlin (rising medially in buttock and knee).
   - Each tether gets a slack ("toe") strain, then a stiff branch, following the anchoring-fibre behaviour noted by Li et al. 2013.
   - **Toe strain and stiffness have no measured value.** Fit them (one value per region, a learned parameter, not hand-tuned) so the simulated skin-vs-bone RMSD and Δp_max match the Cereatti 2017 thigh and shank samples, and the CAMS-Knee samples. Record the fit and its source data in E-MAT.
3. **Hard anchors** (zero-length tethers from dermis to bone) along the **medial gluteal fold to the ischium** (Si 2024; ischio-cutaneous ligament). Fade the anchor density laterally. The crease then comes from the anatomy, not from a rule.
4. **Fat bulk:** a nearly incompressible hyperelastic solid with Sommer-type softer shear. Take its constants from E-MAT, not from this note. Check against Mogale 2023: fat thickness loss of about 16 % (MRI, 30 % BW) and about 41 % (seated US).
5. **Graphics-side fallback:** if the volumetric DAT slide is too costly on the GTX 1650, use the Li 2013 surface method. Run the skin on the tet surface with a tangential limit Δmax and stickiness ζ, both fitted to the same STA targets.

**Change to the handoff doc** (row "fat ↔ skin", §6b): replace "skin slides only a few mm" with "skin + superficial fat + SM move as one (< ~1 mm slip). Skin moves 7–14 mm RMS, up to 23–72 mm peak, relative to the thigh bone, through deep-fat / fascia sliding, muscle motion and skin stretch".

## 7. Open questions

- There are no measured septa stiffness, length or areal density data for the thigh, buttock or knee. Could cadaver ultrasound or the Li & Ahn Table 1 values fill this?
- How much of STA is skin **sliding** and how much is skin **stretch** or muscle wobble? The STA datasets mix them. DIC skin-strain fields (Newman group) could separate stretch.
- No knee-crease septa anatomy and no dimple-depth data were found.
- Wilke's measurement frame (probe on skin) suggests the skin–SF coupling is tight. Confirm by reading the full paper.
- The data licence of the Cereatti supplementary files and CAMS-Knee terms need checking before use. The project is public, so do not redistribute the data.
- The elf is 0.98 m tall and stylised. How should septa density and fat thickness scale from adult data? This needs a scaling rule taken from data, not one chosen by hand.
