Bones and muscles inside a generated 3D figure

One textured 3D model of a character goes in. A full skeleton, about 680 muscle and tendon pieces, and the nose's cartilages come out, fitted inside her own surface.

Open the interactive 3D viewer Code on GitHub

What you are looking at

The figure is a fictional elf, generated as a single textured mesh about 98 cm tall. Nothing inside it was modelled by hand. The viewer lets you turn it, fade the body, switch muscles and bones on and off, and cut it open.

The video above is a recording of that viewer being turned, not a separate render.

How it is made

  1. Read the model. Eight rendered views, per-point colours and normals.
  2. Find joints and landmarks. A whole-body keypoint detector on the renders, triangulated to 3D; close-ups for hands, feet and the face.
  3. Tell skin from cloth and hair. SAM 3 masks voted onto the surface; a vision language model labels each piece.
  4. Mark what hangs loose. A decision model (Jev) boxes loose clothing and the philtrum on the renders. Hidden parts of her back are stood in for by her own mirror image.
  5. Place an anatomy atlas. The Z-Anatomy atlas is placed by named points, then each bone is fitted with joints held together and bones kept inside the body.
  6. Fit the skull. A convex problem bends the skull under her face using tissue depths measured on 388 CT scans, with the nose's cartilages in the fit.
  7. Carry the muscles. Muscles follow the bones they lie on, are brought inside her body, set to the right depth under the skin, and repaired where anything pokes out.

What is still wrong

  • Two small throat muscles are badly misplaced, and about 16% of muscle points overlap bone.
  • The back near the shoulder blades is crumpled, and the forearms under the arm warmers are 2–4 mm too thick.
  • The upper jaw is stretched about 17% under the philtrum, more than any real skull, so that the bone follows her stylised face.
  • The pipeline still contains numbers set by hand. They are listed, with what should replace each, in the open list.
  • Only this one figure has been run end to end.
